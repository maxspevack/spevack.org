# Privacy Policy

*Effective 2026-11-01. Supersedes the 2026-10-31 version, which said all of this with a straight face.*

This page exists because Google requires one. Google requires one because somewhere a form has a mandatory field labeled "Privacy policy URL," and the form does not care that the application it protects has exactly one user, who is also its author, its operator, its data subject, its data controller, its data processor, and the person typing this sentence. So: a privacy policy. By me. About me. Addressed to me. Reviewed by Google and found adequate, which is the nicest thing anyone has said about it.

It covers **Max Workspace**, the Google OAuth application behind the personal tools Max Spevack runs on his own computers, and nothing else on this site. The rest of spevack.org is a résumé and a photograph. They have no privacy policy. They are fine.

## What the tools are

Max Workspace is not a product. It is not a service. It is not a startup, a platform, an ecosystem, or a journey. It is the credential that lets two programs Max wrote or operates for himself read and write his own Google account:

- a Model Context Protocol server, so his AI tooling can see his Calendar, Gmail, and Drive instead of asking him to paste things into it;
- a to-do list that mirrors itself onto a calendar he owns, because he likes seeing his chores next to his meetings.

That is the whole roster. There is no waitlist.

## Whose data

Max's. Only Max's. The application is authorized against his Google account and no other. It has one user, and he is also the operator. If this policy has a target audience, it is a man reading about himself.

If you are not Max and are reading this because you reached the app's consent screen: something has gone wrong, you should click the other button, and you should probably tell him how you got there.

## What is accessed, and why

Calendar, Gmail, and Drive data in Max's account, read and written on his instruction, for his own use. Nothing is collected for analytics, advertising, profiling, or training. No model learns from this data, personalized or otherwise. There is no dashboard. Nobody is deriving insights. The data is accessed because Max asked a program to go get it, and it went and got it.

## Storage and sharing

Data stays in Max's Google account and on the machines Max owns. Nothing is copied to third parties, sold, rented, pooled, enriched, or shared, because there is no one on the other end of that sentence. The OAuth tokens live in the operating system's encrypted credential store on each machine, which is a more careful arrangement than many companies with actual customers manage.

## Retention and deletion

Nothing is retained anywhere except Max's Google account, which already had it, and Max's own disks, which he controls. To end the arrangement, Max revokes the app at [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions) and deletes the tokens from his credential store, which takes about as long as reading this paragraph. If you want your data deleted from Max Workspace, you are Max, and you know where it is.

## Google Limited Use

Use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the [Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy), including the Limited Use requirements. This is the one sentence on this page Google actually reads. It is reproduced without irony, which is the most respectful thing this page does.

## Changes to this policy

If what the tools access, or how they use it, ever changes, this page changes first and the user is told. The user has been told. He wrote it.
